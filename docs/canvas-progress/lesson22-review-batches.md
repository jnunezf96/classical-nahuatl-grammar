# Lesson 22 atom-job review batches

All 251 atoms are included exactly once. Unaccepted groups remain proposals; implementation credit appears only after accepted jobs pass their exact checks.

## Batch 1, Group 1: Inherently impersonal VNCs and fictitious subjects

- Sections: §22.1
- Atoms: 21
- Proposed direction: 12 both; 9 reading only
- Writing job: Build an inherently impersonal VNC from any entered intransitive Source whose typed lexical analysis is selected, use known Canvas stems only as defaults and examples, force the empty third-person singular subject frame, and realize ordinary tense and sentence material without creating a personal subject.
- Reading job: Show that the VNC is agentless but not subjectless, identify the grammatical subject as a nonreferential fiction, and present the licensed meteorological or event reading without treating an English gloss as grammar.
- User/application split: The user supplies the lexical stem and ordinary tense or mood. The application recognizes an inherently impersonal stem and fixes its subject automatically; there is no personal-versus-impersonal switch for a stem that can occur only impersonally.
- Control policy: Reuse stem, tense, mood, polarity, and sentence controls; do not add fictitious-subject, third-person, agentless, meteorological, or supplementability switches.
- Proposal: Recognize the exact inherently impersonal lexical stems, keep their grammatical subject fixed as third-person singular, and distinguish a fictitious subject from both a real referent and a missing subject.
- Status: ACCEPTED
- Exactly observed: 21/21

## Batch 1, Group 2: Nonanimate subjects are not impersonal subjects

- Sections: §22.2
- Atoms: 16
- Proposed direction: 10 both; 6 reading only
- Writing job: Preserve the third-person singular surface shared by both constructions while using typed referent and supplementation structure to keep a nonanimate common-number subject personal and an empty impersonal subject unsupplementable.
- Reading job: Show whether the subject refers to one or more nameable nonanimate entities or to no entity at all, and explain why visible singular morphology does not erase common number or authorize an impersonal reading.
- User/application split: The user supplies an actual subject supplement or referential context when one is intended. The application combines that structure with lexical Source authority; merely omitting a visible supplement never turns an ordinary nonanimate subject into an impersonal one.
- Control policy: Reuse subject supplement and referent cues; do not add singular-versus-common-number, nameability, supplementability, or nonanimate-versus-impersonal switches.
- Proposal: Keep a specific nonanimate common-number subject separate from a truly impersonal subject: both use third-person singular morphology, but only the nonanimate subject has an identifiable referent and can be supplemented.
- Status: ACCEPTED
- Exactly observed: 16/16
