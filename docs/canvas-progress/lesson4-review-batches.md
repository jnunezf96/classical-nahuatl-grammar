## Batch 1, Group 1: What a nuclear clause is and where it can be used

- Sections: §4.1
- Atoms: 21
- Proposed direction: 8 both; 13 reading only
- Writing job: Build every non-particle vocable as a complete nuclear clause with a subject and a predicate, and allow that clause to serve as a sentence, main clause, dependent clause, or conjoined clause.
- Reading job: Help the reader recognize a nuclear clause as a clause rather than an English-style word and recover its subject and predicate.
- User/application split: The user supplies the intended content and construction; the application keeps non-particles inside nuclear-clause structure and supplies the required subject-predicate frame.
- Control policy: Do not add a word-versus-clause control. Particle versus nuclear-clause status follows the entered or selected source, while sentence and clause use reuses the normal sentence and clause choices.
- Proposal: Make nuclear-clause identity a foundation of every normal VNC and NNC Result, while keeping Andrews's terminology available to the reader.
- Status: ACCEPTED
- Exactly observed: 21/21

## Batch 2, Group 6: Personal pronouns and reference

- Sections: §4.6
- Atoms: 66
- Proposed direction: 45 both; 21 reading only
- Writing job: Build personal pronouns only as affixes in the authorized person, number, objective, and possessive positions; preserve person, animacy, humanness, number, and case; leave contextual reference and English gender to interpretation.
- Reading job: Guide the reader through affixal pronouns, third-person ambiguity, common number, cultural animacy, case, deixis, anaphora, and cataphora.

- Control policy: Reuse participant, person, number, animacy, humanness, object, possessor, and context choices only when they change grammar or meaning. Do not add gender, deixis terminology, or anaphora terminology as required controls.
- Proposal: Make the five pronoun categories govern normal VNC and NNC behavior while keeping contextual interpretation in the reading guide.
